Donors are moving more funding to local partners. That is good news for Somali organizations, but it comes with a quiet test. Before anyone calls you, a program officer has already looked you up. In a few minutes they decide whether you look like a partner they can defend to their own board.

Here is what they check.

## 1. Your website

Does it load on a phone? Does it say clearly who you are, where you work and what you have achieved? A missing or broken website is the fastest way to lose a donor you never knew was looking.

## 2. Your registration and governance

Registration details, board members and policies should be easy to find. Due diligence teams look for them first.

## 3. Your results, in numbers

Not activities. Results. How many people, in which districts, with what change. If your numbers live only in a spreadsheet on one laptop, donors cannot see them.

## 4. Your stories

Numbers prove scale. Stories prove change. Donors look for real people, filmed and photographed with consent and dignity.

## 5. Your reports

A clear, well designed annual or program report signals a well run organization. A dense, unformatted document signals the opposite.

## 6. Your data systems

Can you show results at any time, not only at reporting deadlines? A live dashboard answers that question before it is asked.

## 7. Your safeguarding and consent practice

How you treat the people in your stories tells donors how you treat the people in your programs.

## 8. Your past partners

Who has trusted you before? Name them, with permission.

## 9. Your team

Real names, real roles, real photos. Donors fund people they can picture.

## 10. Your consistency

The same name, logo and message across your website, reports, social media and proposals. Inconsistency reads as disorganization.
